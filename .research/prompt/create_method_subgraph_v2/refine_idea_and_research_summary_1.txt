# Novelty
ADLAS introduces four technical ideas that are not present in the closest samplers: (1) it adjusts the neighbour budget k online for every layer according to a quantitative ratio of gradient-norm to attention-drift, whereas prior works either keep k fixed (GraphSAINT, SAG-LS) or adapt only the sampling distribution but not the sample count (Bandit-BS, RSC); (2) it adds a lightweight control-variate that re-uses occasional micro full-batch computations and is proved to reduce variance by (1−ρ) – existing GNN samplers use stale messages (SAG-LS) or importance weights but no principled control-variate; (3) it couples the sampler with a GPU power monitor to down-scale k while respecting a variance bound, a capability absent from all listed works; (4) the alias-reservoir data structure lowers O(√n) DepRound cost to expected O(1) without losing unbiasedness, which is a new engineering contribution.  Together these components create an auto-tuning, energy-aware, layer-wise sampler that differs materially from variance-bandit, graph-sampling and sparse-approximation methods in the related literature.

# Significance
Academically, ADLAS tackles two open issues acknowledged by recent literature: the lack of layer-wise variance control after SAG-LS and the absence of control-variate techniques for attentive GNNs. Empirical results on OGB-Products and Papers100M show 1.3× speed-up, 25 % variance drop and the ability to train 10-layer GATs on a single GPU—something existing sampling methods struggle to do. Societally, the energy-aware auto-tuner directly targets the carbon and cost footprint of large-graph training and enables single-GPU or even CPU users to work with OGB-large data, addressing the “democratisation” concern cited in the proposal. While gains are moderate compared with full-batch alternatives and the method still needs occasional full-batch passes, the combination of accuracy parity, energy reduction and zero manual hyper-parameter search makes the contribution practically useful and likely to influence future sampler design.

    "Methodology": "The core methodology involves reformulating the sampling variance optimization as an adversary bandit problem.

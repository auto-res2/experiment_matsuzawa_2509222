# Experiment Strategy
--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 1 – Variance-aware neighbour budgeting
Objective:
Quantify how the hierarchical, per-node budget k̂ in SHANS reduces gradient variance and sampling cost while maintaining accuracy.

Setup:
• Hardware: 1× NVIDIA-A100 (80 GB).
• Datasets & models: OGB-products (10-layer GAT) and OGB-arxiv (6-layer GAT).
• Compared methods:   1) SHANS (full)   2) SHANS – no k̂ (k fixed to kmax)   3) ADLAS   4) Full-batch (oracle).
• Metrics: test accuracy, gradient-estimator variance (trace over last 100 minibatches), neighbours fetched / node, wall-time/epoch.

Procedure:
1. Warm-start each model with identical weights for 5 epochs (full-batch) so training noise is comparable.
2. Train for 50 further epochs with each sampler, logging metrics every 100 iterations.
3. Repeat 3 random seeds.

Expected outcome & demonstration:
• Hierarchical k̂ lowers variance by ≥30 % vs ADLAS and by ≥15 % vs SHANS-no-k̂.
• Neighbours fetched / node drop from kmax=25 to ≈11 on 80 % of nodes.
• Same or better test accuracy (Δ≤0.2 %) with 1.4× faster wall-time.
→ Demonstrates that per-node variance estimates drive a more efficient, theoretically grounded sampling budget without hurting convergence.
--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 2 – Energy-aware mixed-precision & refresh scheduling
Objective:
Show that (i) mixed-precision shipping and (ii) the energy-aware control-variate refresh jointly maximise accuracy/Joule under power caps.

Setup:
• Hardware: Jetson-Nano (10 W cap) and A100 with software cap set to 125 W.
• Dataset/model: Reddit, 4-layer GraphTransformer ( ⇢ attentive and memory heavy).
• Variants:   a) SHANS full   b) SHANS – INT8 off   c) SHANS – energy-scheduler off (fixed R)   d) ADLAS.
• Metrics: accuracy, watt-hours/epoch (NVML / INA219), PCI-e traffic, bit-width histogram, total training time to 92 % validation accuracy.

Procedure:
1. For each variant, sweep three power caps (10 W, 15 W, 20 W on Jetson; 125 W, 175 W, 250 W on A100).
2. Log instantaneous power, selected bit-width, and R schedule.
3. Terminate when validation accuracy reaches pre-set target.

Expected outcome & demonstration:
• Mixed-precision halves PCI-e traffic and yields ≈25–30 % energy savings with no accuracy loss.
• Energy-scheduler lengthens R when marginal gain/Joule is low, cutting further 10 % energy vs fixed R.
• On Jetson-Nano only SHANS full hits 92 % acc within 10 W; ADLAS stalls due to OOM/power throttle.
→ Confirms SHANS’ unique ability to respect strict energy envelopes while preserving model quality.
--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 3 – CPU-only scalability on 10^8-edge web graph
Objective:
Validate that Hutchinson control-variates allow SHANS to scale on CPU-only hardware without micro full-batch passes becoming the bottleneck.

Setup:
• Hardware: dual-socket EPYC server, 2 TB RAM (GPU disabled).
• Dataset: ‘uk-2005’ (15 M nodes, 135 M edges).
• Model: 6-layer GAT (8 heads, 64-d); batch size 10 k.
• Baselines: GraphSAINT-GAT, SAG-LS, ADLAS.
• Variants: SHANS full vs SHANS – Hutchinson off (exact full-batch refresh).
• Metrics: wall-clock time to reach 55 % validation accuracy, RAM peak, CPU utilisation, number of sparse-matrix–vector multiplies per refresh.

Procedure:
1. Pin all methods to 64 CPU threads; disable MKL multithreading inside PyTorch to keep counts fair.
2. Train each variant for 30 epochs, recording all metrics.
3. Profiling: use linux perf to sample function-level CPU time.

Expected outcome & demonstration:
• SHANS full reaches 55 % val acc in ≈4 h; GraphSAINT needs ≈9 h, ADLAS ≈7 h.
• Removing Hutchinson increases SHANS runtime by >40 % owing to dense passes.
• perf shows <8 % of total time spent in control-variate step vs >25 % in baselines’ dense ops.
→ Shows that low-cost Hutchinson estimates eliminate the remaining bottleneck for CPU-bound, billion-edge training, underlining SHANS’ hardware-agnostic scalability.
